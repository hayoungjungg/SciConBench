SciConBench

Benchmarks go stale. This one doesn't.

Every static benchmark built from published literature ends up inside the next model's training corpus. SciConBench sidesteps that by drawing questions from systematic reviews as they are published — and by re-running the full frontier every month, so the leaderboard tracks capability rather than memorization.

Each month the pipeline discovers newly published Cochrane reviews, decomposes their expert conclusions into atomic facts, and asks every tracked model the clinical question those reviews set out to answer. Retrieval runs through a clean-room proxy that hides the review itself, anything citing it, and anything published after it.

How scoring works

Long-form conclusions can't be graded by string match. SciConBench grades them fact by fact.

Factual precision

The agent's conclusion is decomposed into atomic facts, and a judge labels each one supported, contradicted, or not supported against the full review. Contradictions are penalized twice — once for not being supported, once for being wrong.

(supported / total) × ((total − contradicted) / total)
Factual recall

The expert conclusion is decomposed the same way, and the judge checks how many of those facts the agent's conclusion actually conveys. This is what separates a confident-sounding summary from a complete one.

supported expert facts / total expert facts
Clean room

All retrieval is proxied. The harness blocks the target review, any document citing it, and anything published after its date, across web search, academic search, and page fetches — so the answer cannot simply be looked up.
